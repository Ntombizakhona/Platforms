Exam Preparation and Hands-On Cloud Learning
--------------------------------------------

When it comes to learning cloud computing, artificial intelligence, and other emerging technologies, I have always believed that understanding the theory is only half the journey. You can watch countless tutorials, read documentation, and prepare for certification exams, but nothing quite compares to getting your hands dirty and actually working with the technology.

This is where QA Platform currently stands out… in the world of AI.

I’ve been using the platform, and so far, I really like what it offers, particularly for exam preparation and hands-on learning.

### Watching and Listening

One of the things I appreciate about QA Platform is its video content.

Sometimes, you don’t necessarily want to read through pages of documentation or follow lengthy written tutorials. Being able to watch instructional videos and, importantly, listen to them makes learning more flexible.

I particularly enjoy the ability to listen to the videos. It makes it easier to consume learning material while doing other things, rather than having to dedicate all your attention to reading.

It’s a small feature, but one that makes a difference to the overall learning experience.

### Doing Hands-On Labs

> _❤️_**_My favourite feature_**

The hands-on labs are probably what I appreciate most about QA Platform.

Cloud computing is inherently practical. You need to understand how services work, how they interact, how to configure them, and how to troubleshoot problems.

Reading about virtual machines, networking, storage, containers, or cloud security is useful, but actually deploying and configuring these services takes your understanding to another level.

The labs provide an opportunity to move beyond theory and gain practical experience in a structured learning environment.

For anyone preparing for cloud certifications, this is particularly valuable. You aren’t just memorising concepts to answer examination questions. You’re developing skills that you can potentially apply in real-world environments.

### Preparing for Certifications

Another reason I find QA Platform useful is its exam preparation content.

Certifications provide a structured way to approach learning, especially when exploring a new technology or cloud ecosystem.

Having learning material, instructional videos, and practical labs within the same environment creates a more complete preparation experience.

Rather than jumping between multiple platforms to find theory, videos, practice material, and practical exercises, you have an opportunity to bring several aspects of your learning together.

### QA Platform in the Age of AI

Something I’ve noticed about the current AI learning landscape is that many AI-powered learning experiences are still **heavily** _text-oriented._

AI tools are excellent at explaining concepts, generating study notes, creating quizzes, summarising documentation, and answering technical questions.

However, there is still a gap between explaining how something works and providing an environment where you can actually work with it.

Not every AI learning tool integrates instructional videos, and hands-on lab environments are not always available.

This is why I think platforms like QA Platform remain particularly valuable.

AI can explain how to deploy a cloud resource, generate the commands, and walk you through the configuration. But having an actual lab where you can execute those commands, observe the results, make mistakes, and troubleshoot is a different learning experience altogether.

The future of technical learning shouldn’t be a choice between AI, video-based learning, and hands-on labs. It should bring them together.

### My Final Redundant Verdict

I’m still exploring QA Platform, but my experience so far has been positive.

What makes it stand out for me is the combination of:

*   **Video-based learning**: The ability to watch and listen to instructional content.

> **_What About Udemy? Well, I kind of used to recommend Udemy, but they got acquired by Coursera, and honestly there’s way too many instructors and courses there, which makes it difficult to choose the right course especially for beginners. So, I won’t be recommending Udemy for a while._**

*   **Hands-on labs:** Practical opportunities to experiment with the technologies you’re learning.
*   **Exam preparation:** Structured learning to support certification goals.
*   **Practical understanding:** An opportunity to move beyond memorising concepts and actually apply them.

**_I particularly appreciate the labs and the ability to listen to videos._** Those two features make the platform a worthwhile addition to my cloud learning toolkit.

As someone interested in cloud computing and AI, I want learning platforms that don’t just tell me what a technology does but give me the opportunity to explore how it works.

And that’s exactly why QA Platform has caught my attention.

Because ultimately, the goal isn’t just to pass the certification exam. It’s to develop the skills to build, deploy, configure, and manage the technology behind it.

My learning philosophy:

Learn by reading.

Learn by watching.

Learn by doing.

Learn by building.

**And, when necessary, learn by getting things wrong.**
